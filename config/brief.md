# Investment brief

This is what the agent looks for. Claude reads this file on every run and scores each hotel against it.
Write in plain language. The next daily run uses whatever is here, and the dashboard shows which version of the brief each assessment used.

The "nice to have" and "red flags" lists are starting assumptions. Change them to match your strategy.

## Must have (anything else is rejected)

- A hotel in France (mainland or Corsica) that is for sale now. Walls and business, business only, or a share sale all count.
- Total asking price no higher than €25,000,000. If the price is "on request", keep the hotel when it could plausibly cost less than that, and say why.
- An operating hotel or hotel-restaurant. A building to convert counts only when the listing says hotel use is allowed or planned.

## Nice to have (raise the score)

- Walls and business (*murs et fonds*) rather than business only.
- Enough rooms to run professionally: roughly 30 or more.
- Demand all year round: large cities, business hubs and well-established tourist areas.
- Room to add value: renovation, repositioning, rebranding or better management.
- Published trading figures (revenue, EBITDA/EBE, occupancy).

## Red flags (lower the score and explain)

- Business-only sale with little time left on the lease.
- Large compliance or renovation needs: fire safety, accessibility, tired rooms.
- Very seasonal trading, flood or other natural hazards, heritage restrictions on works.
- Staff disputes, unpaid debts or legal cases. Insolvency can also be an opportunity, so flag it rather than reject it.

## Scoring guide

- 80–100: strong match. Worth contacting the seller or broker now.
- 50–79: possible match. Worth a closer look.
- Under 50: no match.
